## Chapter 1: Introduction

### 1.1 Project Overview
**Wheyo** is a specialized full-stack ecosystem designed for **The Protein Kitchen**, navigating the intersection of high-performance nutrition and modern food-commerce. The project is bifurcated into two distinct operational layers: an intuitive, macro-centric **Client Interface** and a robust, data-driven **Admin Control Panel**.

### 1.2 The Client Ecosystem
On the consumer side, Wheyo operates as a "Macro-first" pharmacy for food. Unlike traditional food delivery apps, the client interface prioritizes:
- **Nutritional Transparency**: Direct visibility into protein, carb, and fat ratios for every product.
- **Progressive Tracking**: A digital macro-journal (Daily Macros) allowing users to track consumption against personalized protein goals.
- **Seamless Transactions**: A frictionless checkout process integrated with a dynamic coupon engine.

### 1.3 The Admin Mission Control
The repository we are currently examining (Wheyo Admin) serves as the "Nerve Center." It is built for operational excellence and high-density information management.
- **Real-time Oversight**: Live order feeds and business vitals.
- **Product CRM**: Granular control over the protein-to-calorie density of the menu.
- **Growth Engine**: Advanced promotional management via coupon logic with strict validation gates.

## Chapter 2: Literature Survey

### 2.1 The Evolution of Food-Tech
Modern food technology has transitioned from simple "delivery-as-a-service" models to **personalized nutrition-as-a-service**. Our survey of the current landscape reveals:
- **Market Gap**: A lack of integrated platforms that combine *purchasing* with *nutritional analytics*.
- **The Obsidian Trend**: Users are increasingly seeking "Brutalist" and "Technical" interfaces that favor utility over ornamental design—a trend reflected in the Wheyo design language.

### 2.2 Backend Paradigms: Serverless vs. Monolithic
In the context of scaling a medium-sized enterprise like The Protein Kitchen:
- **PostgreSQL & Supabase**: Our research identifies Row-Level Security (RLS) as the gold standard for multi-tenant data safety without the overhead of complex backend middleware.
- **Atomic Operations**: The use of UUID-based primary keys and JSONB for polymorphic data (Order Items) ensures schema-less flexibility with relational integrity.

## Chapter 3: Methodology

### 3.1 Technical Stack (The M.E.S.T. Framework)
We adopted a highly opinionated stack to ensure maximum developer velocity and production-grade stability:
- **M**otion (React): For tactile micro-animations that prevent "UI Deadness."
- **E**sbuild/Vite: For sub-second HMR and optimized production chunking.
- **S**upabase: As a real-time BaaS (Backend-as-a-Service).
- **T**ailwind 4: For a utility-first CSS architecture that remains maintainable.

### 3.2 Development Lifecycle
1. **Architectural Blueprints**: Designing the SQL schema (PostgreSQL) with a focus on RLS (Row Level Security) to isolate user data at the database level rather than the application level.
2. **Component Atomization**: Building the UI using a "Brutalist" design recipe—favoring monospaced fonts (`JetBrains Mono`), high contrast, and data-dense layouts.
3. **Integration & Validation**: Implementing manual chunking strategies in the build pipeline to ensure compatibility with edge-hosting providers like Vercel.
4. **Export Engineering**: Utilizing `jsPDF` for generating human-readable, professional order manifests directly from client-side state.

### 3.3 Security Invariants
The methodology strictly enforces:
- **JWT-based Auth**: Stateless authentication leveraging Supabase.
- **RBAC (Role-Based Access Control)**: Restricting critical operations (Update/Create Product) to verified admin email accounts and internal `admins` lookup tables.
